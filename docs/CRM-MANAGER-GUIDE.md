# Khulisa CRM
## A practical guide for Client Success and Growth Managers

Updated: 12 October 2026

Your job is to bring in suitable clients, keep relationships healthy, make client requests clear, and find useful growth opportunities. The developer handles building, technical decisions, approval of new work and prices, and financial records.

**The working rule: every conversation should end with a recorded outcome and a clear next step.**

## Quick start: the whole workflow

**Capture a lead → understand their needs → follow up → win the work → onboard the client → submit clear requests → keep the client informed → record feedback → follow up on payments → propose useful growth.**

You do not need to repeat every step for an existing client. Start with their current need.

| If you need to… | Go to… |
| --- | --- |
| Decide what to work on today | Dashboard → Today's work |
| Contact a prospect or update their stage | Leads |
| Find a client's full record | Clients → select the client |
| Review check-ins, client health, requests, or opportunities across your clients | Client Success |
| Check project progress | Projects |
| Check an invoice or schedule a payment reminder | Invoices |
| Check your earnings | Commissions |
| Change appearance or request a password reset | Settings → Personal |

## 1. Sign in and find your way around

Use the account provided through your invitation. There is no public sign-up. If you have forgotten your password, use the password reset option on the login page or in Settings.

On a phone, open the menu at the top left to see the main sections. The search bar finds leads, clients, projects, and invoices you are allowed to see. Select a result to open it.

The sun/moon button changes between light and dark appearance. Your profile menu includes Logout.

You see your assigned leads, clients linked to your work, and clients you have been assigned to manage directly. Some projects can be viewed without giving you permission to change their delivery steps. If a record you need is missing, ask the developer to check the assignment.

Your account may be labelled **Agent** in the app. This is the account type used for Client Success and Growth Managers.

## 2. Start and finish each day with a plan

Open Dashboard → **Today's work**. Start with serious client concerns and urgent work, then overdue follow-ups, today's calls, payment follow-ups, missing materials, and items waiting for a decision.

The dashboard also shows your active leads, conversion rate, upcoming lead follow-ups, active projects, and commissions. Conversion rate compares won leads with leads marked Won or Lost; it does not count open leads as completed decisions.

Open **Client Success** for the full client health, check-in, request, and growth lists. Use the filters to focus on a particular group. Where **Load more** appears, use it to see additional records. A dashboard summary is a starting point, not the full list of every record.

Before finishing your day, check that conversations are saved, reminders have correct dates, urgent concerns have been raised, and each open item has a next step.

## 3. Add a new lead

A lead is a business or person who may become a client. Search for their business, contact name, or email first to avoid creating a duplicate. Compare phone numbers on any similar records you find.

Open **Leads**, choose the add-lead button, and enter the details you know. Use real contact details. If information is unknown, leave optional fields blank rather than making it up.

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| Business Name | The business you are speaking to. |
| Contact Name | The person you are dealing with. |
| Phone and Email | Their correct contact details. |
| Source | Where the lead came from: Facebook, Google, Referral, Walk-in, or Other. |
| Stage | Their current position in the sales process. |
| Follow-up Date | The date you need a reminder to contact them. |
| Next action | One clear task you plan to do next. |
| Qualification | Their need, budget, decision-maker, and planned start date. |
| Notes | Helpful background, questions, and conversation details. |

**Next action example:** “Call on Thursday to confirm the website requirements.”

**Qualification example:** “Needs a five-page website for a security company. Budget around R3,500. The director approves the purchase. Wants to launch next month.”

If you have not discussed everything, write: “Interested in a website. Budget, decision-maker, and start date still need to be confirmed.”

**A date written in Next action or Notes does not create a reminder. Set the Follow-up Date as well.**

Estimated value and assignments are controlled by the developer where those fields are restricted.

## 4. Keep the lead stage honest

Use the stage selector in the lead list or board. The stage should reflect what actually happened.

| Stage | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| New | Captured, but no meaningful contact yet. |
| Contacted | You have reached out or started a conversation. |
| Proposal Sent | The client has received the proposal. |
| Negotiation | You are discussing the work, price, or terms. |
| Won | The client has agreed to proceed with the approved work. |
| Lost | The opportunity is no longer moving forward. |

When editing a lost lead, record the **Lost reason**. For example: “Budget is below the approved package price.” A slow reply alone does not mean the lead is lost.

## 5. Complete lead follow-ups and schedule another when needed

Open the lead → **Lead follow-ups**.

1. Set the current follow-up date and choose **Save date**.
2. Contact the prospect when due.
3. Select the contact method: Call, Email, WhatsApp, Meeting, or Note.
4. Write the outcome.
5. If another contact is needed, select **Schedule another follow-up** and choose the next date.
6. Choose **Complete follow-up**.

There is no two-follow-up limit. Each completed follow-up stays in the activity history. Completing without another date clears the reminder; you can schedule a new one later. **Stop reminders** clears the current schedule.

Use the Leads filters for due/overdue, upcoming, or no follow-up date. Won and Lost leads stop receiving acquisition reminders.

Example outcome: “Client wants a proposal emailed today. They will review it with the director. Follow up next Tuesday.”

## 6. Move a won lead into client care

Choosing **Won** in the stage selector for a lead without a linked client opens **Convert to Client + Project?**. Review the handoff details before choosing **Confirm Conversion**.

**Create Project now** starts selected in this dialog. Leave it selected only when the developer has agreed to the package and project. Check the project name and package carefully. If you only need a client record for now, untick it before confirming.

The conversion creates or links the client. Saving a lead as Won through the add/edit form creates or links the client without creating a project. New standalone projects are created by the developer.

After conversion:

1. Open the linked client and confirm the contact details.
2. Review onboarding requirements.
3. Record the agreed work and any missing information.
4. Schedule the first client check-in.
5. Tell the developer about anything that may affect delivery.

Do not create another client because conversion seems slow. Refresh and check the existing record first. Winning a lead does not mean payment has been received.

## 7. Use the client's tabs

Open **Clients**, select the business, and use these tabs:

| Tab | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Overview | Contact details, business information, and relationship summary. |
| Conversations | Check-ins, client activity, and editable message templates. |
| Onboarding | Client health, concerns, last contact, and the materials checklist. |
| Delivery | Linked projects and work requests. |
| Approvals | Client decisions and revision history. |
| Growth | Additional services, renewals, and referrals. |
| Billing | Invoices, recorded payments, outstanding balances, and follow-up links. |

Use **Edit client details** to correct contact or business information. The developer manages contact-manager assignments. Save changes before leaving the page; an unfinished draft is not a saved record.

## 8. Schedule client check-ins and record contact

For an existing client, open **Conversations → Client check-in**.

Choose the **Next contact date**, explain what you need to discuss, and tick **Awaiting client response** when waiting for an answer. Choose **Schedule check-in** or **Update check-in**.

After contacting the client, use **Client activity**:

1. Choose the contact method.
2. Write what happened and what comes next.
3. Add a **Next contact date** only if another follow-up is needed.
4. Mark that you are awaiting a response where appropriate.
5. Choose **Save interaction**.

Leaving the next date blank ends your current check-in reminder. You can also choose **Stop my reminders** in the check-in section.

In Client Success, check-ins can be viewed as Today, Overdue, Awaiting response, Upcoming, or All. “Awaiting response” helps you keep track of a reply; it does not mean a message was automatically sent.

**Useful note:** “Called about the missing logo. Client will email it on Thursday. Check on Friday if it has not arrived.”

## 9. Write notes that are easy to read

Use short paragraphs and clear bullet points. Record facts rather than guesses. Include what happened, what is needed, who acts next, and any agreed date.

The note editor provides **Write**, **Preview**, and **Expand editor**, plus bold, italic, headings, lists, quotes, and links. Use Preview to check formatting before saving. Formatting symbols may appear while writing; the preview shows how the note will read.

CRM notes are for the team. A project's **Client update** is different: it is shown in the client portal.

## 10. Keep client health up to date

Open **Onboarding → Client health**.

| Status | Use it when… |
| --- | --- |
| Healthy | The relationship is stable and there are no major concerns. |
| Needs Attention | Something needs action, such as missing content or delayed replies. |
| At Risk | There is a serious concern, such as possible cancellation or repeated complaints. |

Write a clear **Health reason** and choose **Save health**. If the developer needs to act, use the **Save and escalate…** button. Explain the problem, its effect, and the help needed. The developer records the resolution.

Example: “Needs Attention — product photos are missing, so the next stage cannot begin. Client promised them by Friday.”

Recorded calls, emails, WhatsApp messages, and meetings update the last-contact date. Use the date field for an earlier genuine contact if needed. A routine note is not proof that you spoke to the client.

## 11. Complete the onboarding checklist

In **Onboarding**, track Logo and brand assets, Website content, Access credentials, Signed contract, and Client requirements.

| Checklist status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Outstanding | Still needed. |
| Requested | You have asked the client to provide it. |
| Received | It has been supplied or confirmed. |
| Not Required | It does not apply to this client's work. |

Add helpful notes, then choose **Save checklist**. Do not mark something Received just because the client promised to send it.

For access, record whether access has been provided. Share passwords through the agreed secure method rather than putting them in notes, request attachments, or the portal.

## 12. Turn client needs into clear work requests

Open **Delivery → New request**, or create a request from Client Success.

Enter the client, a clear title, category, priority, request details, and optional project and requested date. Categories are **Website change**, **Issue**, and **New requirement**. Priorities are Low, Normal, High, and Urgent.

Use Urgent for a serious problem that needs immediate attention, not every normal edit. A requested date is not a confirmed deadline until the developer agrees.

**Example:** “Update opening hours on the contact page. Replace the current hours with Monday–Friday, 08:00–17:00. Client confirmed this by WhatsApp. Requested for Friday.”

You may attach up to **3 files, each up to 2 MB**: PNG, JPG, WebP, PDF, or text. Choose **Submit request**. You can manage attachments on your own eligible requests while they remain open; use the controls shown on that request.

| Request status | What happens next |
| --- | --- |
| Received | The developer reviews the request. |
| In Progress | The work is being done. |
| Ready for Review | Arrange review and collect clear client feedback. |
| Completed | The request is finished. |

The developer changes request progress and adds delivery updates. Creating a request does not approve an extra charge or automatically create a project. Put new paid work through Growth as well.

## 13. Check projects and communicate progress

Open **Projects** or the client's Delivery tab. Check the status, due date, milestones, notes, and linked invoices.

If you are the assigned delivery coordinator, the app allows you to update milestones on an open project and use Not Started, In Progress, On Hold, or Waiting for Client. Only mark a milestone done when the work is genuinely ready.

The developer confirms **Completed** or **Delivered** and reopens closed projects. Completing the last milestone yourself does not make the project finally completed.

A dedicated client contact manager may view a project without having permission to change another coordinator's milestones, status, or client update. Send a request to the developer when those controls are unavailable.

Where **Client update** is available, write a short, accurate progress message and choose **Publish update**. It appears in the portal, so avoid private team comments. Do not promise an unconfirmed delivery date.

## 14. Record feedback and approvals

Open **Approvals → Record feedback**. Record the title, **Deliverable version**, decision, date, client approver/contact, and notes.

The decisions are **Approved**, **Rejected**, and **Changes Requested**. Identify exactly what was reviewed and where the feedback came from.

Example: “Home page v2 approved by the director by email on 12 October. Contact page still needs review.”

Choose **Save feedback revision**. If the decision changes later, use **Record next revision**. Review the history instead of replacing the earlier decision.

Recording feedback here keeps a record. It does not automatically complete a project or every related request. Tell the developer what needs to happen next.

## 15. Help clients use the portal

The developer creates the private portal link, manages shared files, and publishes the revision ready for review. Ask for the correct link before sending it to the client.

Clients can view shared progress, milestones, team updates, available files or folder links, missing-material labels, and published review instructions. They can submit a decision for the current published revision or send a change request.

Their response starts as **pending verification**. The developer confirms it before recording it as official feedback or a work request. A verified review decision can update the specifically linked request; it does not automatically finish the whole project.

Explain what the client should review and follow up if needed. Keep the link private. If it has expired or stopped working, ask the developer for help. Final files remain available after delivery while the link remains valid.

The portal does not provide general client file uploads. Use the agreed folder or contact method for materials. New client requests may still need approval of the work and price.

**Message to send with the link:**

> Hi [Client Name], here is your Khulisa Media client portal: [Portal Link]. You can view project progress, access shared files, and send feedback or change requests. We will let you know when something is ready for review. Please keep this link private, and let me know if you need help.

## 16. Propose growth that benefits the client

Open **Growth** on the client or the growth queue in Client Success. Choose **Propose opportunity**.

Select **Additional service**, **Renewal**, or **Referral**. Enter the title, proposal, suggested scope, optional price, and optional target or renewal date. Scope means the exact work you suggest doing.

Example: “Add a product catalogue so customers can view available items before contacting the business. Suggested scope: one catalogue page with ten products. Price to be confirmed by the developer.”

Choose **Submit growth proposal** and follow its progress:

| Status | Your next step |
| --- | --- |
| Proposed | Wait for the developer's review. |
| Needs Changes | Read the feedback, revise your proposal where allowed, and submit again. |
| Approved | Use the approved work and price when discussing next steps. |
| Declined | Read the reason; do not promise the proposed work. |

The developer approves scope and pricing and can create the linked invoice. After invoicing, changes require a new proposal. Keep growth linked to the existing client rather than creating a duplicate client.

A suggested price is not an approved quote. A referral entry is an opportunity record, not an automatic referral payment.

## 17. Follow up on invoices and payments

Open **Invoices** or **Billing**. Check the invoice number, client, due date, total, amount paid, outstanding balance, and payment history.

| Invoice status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Draft | Prepared but not yet issued to the client. |
| Sent | Issued and awaiting payment. |
| Partially Paid | Some money has been recorded; a balance remains. |
| Overdue | The payment due date has passed. |
| Paid | The invoice has been settled. |

Use **Download / Print Invoice** to print it or save a PDF through the print window. Check with the developer before sending a draft invoice.

On an unpaid invoice, open **Payment follow-up**, choose a date, write the next action or promise to pay, and save it. **Invoices → My payment follow-ups** brings your schedules together. Reschedule after contact or use **Stop reminders**. A settled invoice stops generating payment reminders.

Use **Log client contact** to record the conversation. Pass proof of payment to the developer for checking and recording. Managers cannot create or change invoices, record payments, or mark commission payouts.

## 18. Use templates, then send the message yourself

Open **Conversations → Communication templates**. Choose a check-in, missing-materials message, progress update, or payment reminder. Choose WhatsApp or email. For payment reminders, select the correct unpaid invoice.

Choose **Prepare draft**, edit it, and check the recipient, dates, amounts, subject, and wording. Replace unfinished bracketed instructions. Use the copy or open-message action shown, then send the message yourself in WhatsApp or email.

Changing the template or invoice means you should prepare a fresh draft. Recording an activity, creating a reminder, and preparing a message do not send a client message automatically.

## 19. Understand reminders and notifications

Open the bell menu to read notifications. Select one to open the related record. **Mark all as read** clears the unread indicator. Swipe a notification on a phone, or use its X on desktop, to dismiss it. Dismissing a notification does not stop the underlying reminder.

Use **Enable desktop notifications** in the bell menu to allow notifications; this label may also appear on a phone. If permission is already allowed, the option may say **Enable / retry background notifications**. Then choose **Send test push to my devices**. Also allow notifications in your phone or browser settings. Set this up on each device you use. **Background push registered** means the device has been registered; use the test to check that a notification arrives.

| Reminder | Who receives it | When it stops |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lead follow-up | The assigned agent. | Date is cleared, follow-up finishes without another date, or lead becomes Won/Lost. |
| Client check-in | The manager who scheduled it. | An interaction is saved without another date, or reminders are stopped. |
| Payment follow-up | The person who scheduled it. | Invoice is settled or reminders are stopped. |

Morning background reminders are scheduled for **08:00 South African time**. On the free plan they may arrive during **08:00–09:00**, rather than at exactly 08:00. They begin on the chosen follow-up date and repeat while still due. Opening the CRM can also check due reminders at other times.

These are staff reminders, not automatic messages to clients. If you need preparation days before contact, choose an earlier reminder date. Continue checking your daily queue even when phone notifications are enabled.

## 20. Check commissions and personal settings

Open **Commissions** to see your own earnings. **Pending** means the invoice payment is still needed; **Ready for Payout** means the invoice is paid and the commission has not been paid out; **Paid Out** means the payout was recorded.

The developer controls rates, payment records, and payouts. Ask about a specific invoice if an amount looks wrong. Additional services, renewals, and referrals do not automatically promise a commission without an agreed arrangement.

In **Settings → Personal**, choose light or dark appearance or request a password reset. Shared template policy, team access, pricing rules, company-wide Reports, and maintenance are handled by the developer.

## 21. If something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| A record is missing | Clear filters, refresh, and ask the developer to check your assignment. |
| A list seems incomplete | Use Load more where shown and check the filter. |
| Saving fails | Keep the message or note, check your connection, and retry. Check whether it already saved before making a second record. |
| Access is denied | Sign out and sign in again. If it continues, send the developer the screen name and error. |
| No phone notifications | Check phone/browser permission, open the bell, and send a test push. Share the result with the developer. |
| Portal link fails | Ask the developer to check it or provide a new one. |
| Payment information looks wrong | Share the invoice number and proof with the developer. |

Avoid deleting records to hide a problem. Deletion can be permanent and remove history. Ask the developer before deleting important lead records.

## Your daily checklist

- Check Today's work and the notification bell.
- Handle urgent concerns, overdue contacts, and today's follow-ups.
- Record each conversation and choose another date only when needed.
- Keep client health and onboarding materials accurate.
- Submit clear requests and check delivery updates.
- Arrange client reviews and record exact decisions.
- Follow up on unpaid invoices without changing financial records.
- Propose useful growth without promising unapproved prices or deadlines.
- Finish the day with a clear next step for each open item.

**Remember: a conversation is not a work request; a request is not a price approval; approval is not payment; and delivery is not the end of client care.**
